In almost or study, including [[representation note]], we focous on field $\mathbb{C}$ or algebraically closed field with character 0. Now we will move on to the case of field that are not algebraically closed (for example $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{R}$). 

Now, let $K$ be a field of characteristic 0 and $C$ a algebraical closure of $K$. If $V$ is a field over $K$, let $V_{C}=C\otimes_{K}V$ be the new vector space by changing the base field. Then for a representation $\rho:G\rightarrow GL(V)$ over $K$, we have $\rho_{C}:G\rightarrow GL(V)\rightarrow GL(V_{C})$ over the field $C$, one can also see it as $V_{C}=C[G]\otimes_{K[G]}V$. On the other hand, the character $x_{\rho}$ as well as all class function will remain the same.

We denote by $R_{K}(G)$ the group generated by the characters of the representations of $G$ over $K$; it is a subring of the ring $R(G)=R_{C}(G)$. (or we could define it as the [[Grothendieck group]] for the category of $K[G]$-modules of finite type)

**Proposition**: Let $(V_{i},\rho_{i})$ be the distinct (up to isomorphism) irreducible linear representations of $G$ over $K$, and let $\chi_{i}$ be their character. Then 
1. The $\chi_{i}$ form a basis of $R_{K}(G)$.
2. The $\chi_{i}$ are mutunally orthogonal.

*Proof*: It is clear that $\chi_{i}$ generate $R_{K}(G)$, and one notice that $dim_{K}Hom^{G}(V,W)=dim_{C}Hom^{G}(V_{C},W_{C})=\langle \chi_{V},\chi_{W} \rangle$, and the proof is finished.
